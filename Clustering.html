Clustering

2026-06-16

Clustering takes a set of objects, described by features and adds them to groups. Clustering algorithms will always find clusters even if there is none, so it is important to validate the clusters

How to measure similarity

which features? should we transform the features? Euclidean distance:
square root of the sum of squares of the differences between two points

Manhattan distance: Sum of the absolute distances in all coordinates

Hamming distance: counts the number of differences between two strings

Jaccard distance: To measure the dissimilarity between two sets. It is calculated by subtracting the Jaccard similarity from 1. Result between 0 (sets are identical) and 0 (no overlap)

Correlation based distance: Two vectors are similar, if their correlation is high

Nor only vectors and numbers can be compared over distance, also complex or distances in Phylogeny (the leaves of a tree)

Nonparametric mixture detection k-Mean

Main choice: the number of clusters k

PAM: 1. Randomly pick k distinct cluster centers out of the observations 2. Assign each observation to the group to whose center it is the closest 3. Choose a new center for each group by minimizing the distances of all group members to the new center -> Repeat step 2 & 3 until convergence -> for each implementation, differnt groups will appear (random start)

k-means works the same but uses euclidean distance, PAM uses different measurement of distance k-means is therefore sensitive to outliers

# Pam or k-means example

Dansity based clustering (DB Scan)

This method looks for regions of high density seperated by sparse regions (concept of density-reachability) The advantage is being able to cope with clusters that are not convex Groups of point must have at least Minpts points to count as a cluster

Hirarchical clustering

Bottom-up approach, can be split in two approaches: Dendrogram ordering: horizontal distances and orders are usually meaningless, vertical ones encode information Top-Down hierarchies: split all the objects according to chosen criterion -> sort heterogeneous gruops into more homogeneous subgroups

How to compute (dis)siimilarities between aggregated clusters

single linkage: computes the distance between clusters as the smallest distance between two point in the two clusters complete linkage: defines the distance between two points between the clusters wirh the largest distance average linkage: distance between pairs of points between two clusters Ward’s method: the goal is to minimize the variance within the clusters (results in smaller clusters most of the time)

Assesing the quality of clustering result

Within-Group sum of square (and ellbow method to find an appropriate value for k) Calinski-Harabasz index: compares the between group sum of square with the within-group sum of square Gap-statistic: is the cluster real or by coincidence? How fast drops the internal distance with the increase of k? This helps choosing the best k.

Cluster validation with bootstrap

Taking random subsamples of the data and compare the different resulting clusters The between-cluster distance is measured by Euclidean dissimilarity

Clustering as a means for denoising

Remove technical noise from data (for example with DADA)

Wo hätte man Clustering in den bisherigen Aufgaben einsetzen können?

  1. Heatshock experiment: which genes are upregulated (maybe even differentiate between immediately (20 min) and secondly (30 min)), which ones downregulated and which ones uneffected in expression?

  2. To identify functional groups (genes which are up- or downregulated at the same time may have a role in the same function, e.g. same metabolic pathway)

  3. Clustering the different experimental trials timepoint within one trial/ condition -> Dendrogramm for quality check, if the Datapoints will cluster within their expected one.

Deine Begründung für das Bereinigen (Clean): Ja, wir müssen Gene herausfiltern, die in allen Proben den Wert Null haben (also gar nicht gemessen wurden). Da sie sich überhaupt nicht verändern, besitzen sie keine Varianz und würden bei der mathematischen Berechnung (Division durch die Standardabweichung) zu Fehlern führen.

Deine Begründung für das Skalieren (Scale): Ja, das ist absolut überlebenswichtig! In biologischen Systemen gibt es riesige Gene (z.B. Haushaltsgene), die Expressionswerte in den Zehntausenden haben. Gleichzeitig gibt es regulatorische Gene mit Werten von unter 50, die sich aber bei Stress vervierfachen. Wenn du nicht skalierst, misst die PCA am Ende nur die absolute Menge der riesigen Gene. Durch das Skalieren (Mittelwert auf 0, Varianz auf 1) wird die relative Veränderung jedes einzelnen Gens mathematisch gleich stark gewichtet.

# Log Transformation and long format
yeast_log_profiles <- yeast_data %>%
  filter(gene_id %in% top_loadings_genes) %>%
  mutate(across(starts_with("25_") | starts_with("42_"), ~ log2(.x + 1))) %>%
  pivot_longer(
    cols = starts_with("25_") | starts_with("42_"),
    names_to = "Sample",
    values_to = "Log2_Expression"
  ) %>%
  mutate(
    Timepoint = case_when(
      str_starts(Sample, "25_0") ~ "0 min",

      Timepoint == "10 min" ~ 10,
